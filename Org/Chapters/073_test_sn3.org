** Scenario 3: I2P
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: eval_sn3
:END:

This scenario evaluates the anonymity layer end-to-end and quantifies the performance cost relative to that of direct QUIC. Additionally, to guarantee that only two nodes are necessary to establish a DHT connection, {{{mark(bootstrap)}}} serves two roles: {{{mark(bootstrapping)}}} and {{{mark(seeding)}}}. As such, two nodes will be routed through I2P via a local SAM bridge, as described in [[#][Section X.X]], demonstrating that the anonymity layer encapsulates the underlying QUIC protocol and requires no code-level modifications. To keep the I2P measurement tractable, given the increased latency of garlic-routed transfers, a reduced ~{{{mark(10 MB)}}} across 21 pieces is used to complete a full end-to-end download between the two counterparts.

As described in the testbed configuration, the {{{mark(i2pd)}}} instance is deployed on a *Podman* container and configured to expose a SAM bridge at {{{mark(0.0.0.0:7656)}}}. With the I2P client running, both {{{mark(bqti)}}} instances are launched in I2P mode via the {{{mark(--i2p)}}} flag. Upon startup, each node establishes a TCP control connection to the SAM bridge, which is maintained for the node's entire lifespan. This control session negotiates a {{{mark(DATAGRAM)}}} session, binding a local UDP socket to the SAM bridge, responsible for intercepting QUIC traffic and carrying it over to I2P. Additionally, the control session is responsible for converting from {{{mark(.b32.i2p)}}} addresses over to full base64 destination addresses, thereby enabling I2P to route traffic to their respective locations within the network.

After the tunnel is established, the {{{mark(leecher)}}} completes a full admission sequence over I2P, thereby requesting the challenge, computing the {{{gls(pow)}}}, and acquiring a signed token upon submission, entirely through the garlic-routed transport. Nonetheless, every protocol layer operated correctly over the abstraction, thereby proxying all traffic between QUIC and I2P over the UDP DATAGRAM session and the SAM bridge. It is worth noting that, just like standard QUIC in I2P mode, the same QUIC control stream and bidirectional streams per-piece requests are maintained and managed by the destination mapper, proxied through a custom *quinn endpoint*. As a result, this proves that the anonymity layer is fully composable, abstracting away the inner transport layer, and preserving QUIC's characteristics over I2P traffic, but with a high performance overhead.

Considering the test results, the user must decide whether to accept these constraints and whether the latency and long download times are worth the anonymity gained.

*** Performance Evaluation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: eval_sn3_performance
:END:

To evaluate the performance differences between both direct QUIC and QUIC-over-I2P, the same {{{mark(10 MB)}}} payload was transferred both direct QUIC and QUIC-over-I2P on the same hardware.

| Transport          | Tunnel/Conn setup | Transfer (10 MB) | Effective rate |
|--------------------+-------------------+------------------+----------------|
| Direct QUIC (LAN)  | < 1 s             | [X] s            | [Y] MB/s       |
| QUIC over I2P      | ~30 s + warmup    | 578 s (total)    | ~17 KB/s       |
| QUIC over I2P*     | (steady-state)    | ~2.5 s           | ~3.8 MB/s      |

This evaluation confirms that while the integration of QUIC over I2P is successful and maintains full protocol compatibility, it introduces a significant performance-to-anonymity trade-off.

By routing QUIC through I2P results in a substantial reduction in throughput from [X] MB/s to approximately 17 KB/s, primarily due to the cumulative latency of garlic-routed multi-hop routing and the overhead of the SAM datagram framing. For long-lived transfer sessions, results in recurrent stalls and possible connection loss due to periodic tunnel rotation. Additionally, further evaluation identified a critical distinction between the adopted stream model: the unidirectional control stream, carrying DHT and PEX traffic, remains functional under high-latency conditions. While the bidirectional piece-exchange streams are significantly impacted by stream creation and subsequent data transfer for each piece request, resulting in small, contained bursts of 10 to 15 pieces between long-lived intervals, which can make the download take up to several hours.

Under the established threat model in [[#][Section X.X]], this overhead must be considered by the user to determine whether it is an acceptable trade-off, sacrificing performance in exchange for anonymity. The current implementation proves that a secure, anonymous BitTorrent swarm is technically viable, but with the overhead inherent to I2P. Nevertheless, the modularity of the {{{mark(bqti)}}} architecture ensures that the anonymity layer remains optional, allowing users to fall back to the full performance of standard QUIC.
